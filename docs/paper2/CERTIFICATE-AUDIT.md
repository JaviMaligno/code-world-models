# Audit: statistical simultaneity of the coverage certificate (review point #14)

**Question asked.** The paper's strongest certificate — `K = 36`, `rho = 0.363`,
`sup_U ||f - f_hat||_inf <= 0.933` (main.tex:347–349) — uses 20,000 Monte-Carlo
rollouts to estimate the worst cell's per-rollout miss probability `p_C`, Hoeffding for
the upper bound, and a chosen partition. Is it a rigorous bound or an empirically
calibrated one?

**Answer: (ii) — rigorous modulo a Monte-Carlo confidence level that the *code* accounts
for correctly but the *prose* never states.** Nothing has to be relabelled and no
headline number changes. Four defects were found: **two reporting gaps** in the `K = 36`
result (the Hoeffding confidence level and the family-wise exposure of the `K` search are
never stated; the `delta` split is never stated), **one substantive mis-statement** in the
adjacent step-`t` density result (the per-cell Wilson level is not what the paper claims,
which shifts three quoted volumes), and **one latent bug** in the validation harness
(benign today, wrong for any future re-run). All numbers below are re-derived by
`scripts/certificate_simultaneity.py` into `results/certificate_simultaneity.json`.

---

## 1. Was the partition fixed before the rollouts were drawn?

**Part (a) (`K = 8`, `rho = 0.600`, bound `1.534`): yes, trivially — there are no
rollouts.** `gate_partition_certificate.py:119–137` is a deterministic exhaustive search
over `(n_y, n_v, n_a)` with `K <= 24`, scoring each by the closed form
`K(1 - 1/K)^M` (`:114–116`) and keeping the smallest `rho` (`:136`). No sample of any
kind enters part (a). The selection is a geometry optimisation, not an inference.

**Part (b) (`K = 36`): the candidate *family* is fixed in advance; the *member* is
selected using the Monte-Carlo sample.**

- The family is generated by `coarsest_for(rho_target)`
  (`gate_partition_certificate.py:204–221`), swept over `rho_target = 2.00` down to
  `0.09` in steps of `0.01` (`:224–232`). That generator is purely geometric —
  `ceil(2A/rho)`, `ceil(2V/rho)`, `ceil(2Y/slack)` — and reads no data.
  Reconstructed: **26 distinct candidate partitions**, `K` from 1 to 2093
  (`family.n_candidates_a_priori`).
- Which member is certified *is* decided against the sample: `:243–270` scores
  candidates coarse-to-fine and keeps the finest that passes (`:265–268`).
- Every candidate is scored on **the same 20,000 rollouts** — `p_miss_per_cell` re-seeds
  `random.Random(args.seed + i)` for `i in range(mc_rollouts)` on every call
  (`:186`), so the stream is byte-identical across candidates (seeds `4242 … 24241`).

So: no post-hoc partition *shape* was invented after looking at the data, but the
*resolution* was chosen by looking at it. That is a multiple-comparison exposure, and
§2 quantifies it.

*One reassuring asymmetry.* The search stops early (`:246`, after 18 candidates tried or
4 consecutive failures). It stopped at 13 of the 26, having failed on `K = 48, 56, 84,
96`. The 13 unexamined candidates are all *finer* than the selected one, and the
selection rule keeps the finest certified — so a candidate wrongly skipped could only
have *strengthened* the certificate. The stopping rule biases the published result
conservative, not optimistic. (It does rely on `fail` being monotone in `rho`, which is
empirical here rather than proven: the candidates are not nested refinements.)

## 2. How is `K` chosen, and what does the multiple comparison cost?

`K` is the largest (finest `rho`) candidate satisfying `K * ub(p_C)^N <= delta/2`, over a
sequence of candidates scored against one shared sample. Numbers:

| | value | source |
|---|---|---|
| candidates in the a-priori family | 26 | `family.n_candidates_a_priori` |
| candidates actually evaluated | 13 (`K` = 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 16, 24, 30, 36, 48, 56, 84, 96) | `family.K_values_evaluated` |
| of those, certified | 9 | `family.n_certified_of_evaluated` |
| per-cell Hoeffding tests over the evaluated candidates | 411 | `family.cell_tests_evaluated` |
| per-cell Hoeffding tests over the whole a-priori family | 9674 | `family.cell_tests_a_priori_family` |

The published radius unions over the `K` cells of **one** partition
(`:252`, `sqrt(log(3K/delta)/(2M))`), not over the family. Recomputing the whole
selection with `delta/3` spread over every `(partition, cell)` pair instead
(`simultaneity_variants`):

| correction | per-test level | worst `p_C` UB | `K·UB^40` | selected `K` | `rho` | bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **published** (`delta/3K`, one partition) | 4.63e-4 | 0.8140 | 0.00956 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |
| Bonferroni over the 13 evaluated (411 tests) | 4.06e-5 | 0.8160 | 0.01057 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |
| Bonferroni over the full family (9674 tests) | 1.72e-6 | 0.8183 | 0.01183 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |
| family × 1000 (stress test) | 1.72e-9 | 0.8226 | 0.01456 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |
| published level, exact-binomial (Clopper–Pearson) | 4.63e-4 | 0.8094 | 0.00763 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |
| family-corrected level **and** Clopper–Pearson | 1.72e-6 | 0.8130 | 0.00913 | **36** | 0.3633 | **0.9329** |

**The selection survives every correction unchanged** (`headline_robustness.
unchanged_under_every_correction = true`). The reason is structural and worth stating in
the paper rather than hiding: Hoeffding's radius grows like `sqrt(log(1/level))`, so
going from 108 tests (one partition's cells) to 9674 (the whole family) — a 90× wider
correction — raises the `p_C` bound by only `0.0043`, and a further 1000× costs `0.0043`
more. Meanwhile the next candidate up (`K = 48`, `rho = 0.333`) misses the `delta/2`
threshold by a factor of 4.5 (`0.111` against `0.025`) — far more than any Bonferroni
factor can bridge in either direction. There is headroom, and the multiple comparison
consumes a small part of it.

The exact-binomial rows answer the complementary question: `0.814` is Hoeffding slack,
not calibration. Clopper–Pearson at the identical level gives `0.809`, and still does
not reach `K = 48`.

## 3. How is `delta = 0.05` split, and is Hoeffding applied per cell?

**Verified, and this is the point at which the review's suspected "real error" does not
exist.**

- **Hoeffding: `delta/3`, Bonferroni'd down to `delta/(3K)` per cell.**
  `gate_partition_certificate.py:252` is `sqrt(log(3*K/delta)/(2*mc_rollouts))`.
  Inverting one-sided Hoeffding, `exp(-2 M t^2) = delta/(3K)`. At `K = 36`,
  `M = 20000`: level `4.6296e-4`, `t = 0.0138545`, which reproduces the published
  `0.813954 - 0.8001` to 1e-15 (`hoeffding_audit.matches_published_t = true`).
  So `p_C <= p_hat_C + t` holds **simultaneously for all 36 cells** with probability
  `1 - delta/3`, and therefore `max_C p_C <= max_C p_hat_C + t` — the selected maximum
  needs no further correction. **The code does not take a single Hoeffding bound on the
  selected maximum.** That would have been an error; it is not what happens.
  (`gate_coverage_dependent.py:114` uses the identical form.)
- **Gate miss: `delta/2`.** `:254–255`, `fail = K * worst_ub^N <= delta/2`.
- **Total spent: `delta/3 + delta/2 = 5delta/6 = 0.04167 <= 0.05`.** So the published
  `0.933` in fact holds at confidence `1 - 0.0417`, slightly *better* than `1 - delta`.
  `delta/6` is left on the table.
- **Paper's arithmetic checks out exactly.** `36 * 0.813954^40 = 0.0095567`, which
  rounds to the quoted `0.010` and is `<= delta/2 = 0.025`. `L = 1.27`,
  `0.01 + 2(1.27)(0.36333) = 0.93287` → `0.933`. `(4.2 - 0.01)/(2 × 0.36333) = 5.766`
  → `5.77`. All in `paper_arithmetic`.
- **Inconsistency worth one clause of prose:** part (a) spends the *whole* `delta` on the
  coverage event (`:128`, `if fail > args.delta`) while part (b) spends `delta/2` on the
  same event. Both are `>= 1 - delta` statements, so neither is wrong, but `1.534` and
  `0.933` are certified against different coverage budgets.

### Independent oracles (do not import the audited code path)

- **Part (a)'s union bound, against the exact probability.** Inclusion–exclusion in exact
  rational arithmetic, `P(some of K equal cells empty after M draws) = sum_j (-1)^(j+1)
  C(K,j) (1-j/K)^M`: at `K = 8, M = 40` the exact value is **0.038038** against the
  published union bound `0.038319` — the union bound is loose by only 0.73%. A
  brute-force 200,000-trial simulation of the same experiment gives **0.03805**,
  confirming the closed form. At `K = 9` the exact value is `0.07939`, still above
  `delta`, so **`K = 8` is the largest admissible partition under the exact probability
  as well as under the union bound** — the cap is the gate, not the proof.
  (`part_a_exact_oracle`.)
- **Hoeffding's inversion**, re-solved by bisection on `exp(-2Mt^2) - level` with no
  closed form: agrees to 2e-18.
- **Hoeffding's conservatism at the operating point**: the exact binomial tail
  `P(Bin(20000, 0.81395) <= floor((0.81395 - t)·20000))` is `3.16e-7`, against the
  `4.63e-4` Hoeffding level it is charged — conservative by three orders of magnitude,
  which is the same slack the Clopper–Pearson row cashes in.
- **My step-t reimplementation reproduces every published level-set cell count exactly**
  at `z = 4` (`step_t_wilson.all_reproduce_published = true`), which is what makes the
  corrected numbers in §4 trustworthy.

### The non-statistical half, checked in passing

The review's question is about simultaneity, but a leak in the geometry would invalidate
the same numbers, so: `rho = max(Delta_y + dt*Delta_v, Delta_v, Delta_a)` is a correct
sup-diameter bound (for two points in a cell, `|x - x'| = |(y - y') + dt(v - v')| <=
Delta_y + dt*Delta_v`), and the binning in `p_miss_per_cell` (`:191–196`) is honest —
strict inequalities `|y| < Y`, `|v| < V`, `|a| < A` put every counted sample inside the
box, and `int((y+Y)/dy)` then lands in `[0, n_y - 1]` without the clamp ever firing, so
the `ceil`/clamp bug that broke the previous generation of this pair is genuinely absent
here (cells are `2Y/n_y` wide by construction, not nominal-`rho` wide). Boundary points of
`U` are discarded by the strict inequalities but still lie in some cell together with a
counted sample, so the `rho`-net conclusion is unaffected.

## 4. Were the step-`t` level sets selected after inspecting results?

The paper claims (main.tex:375) "a Wilson lower bound per cell at level `delta/K` over a
cell family fixed in advance (so selecting a level set afterwards is legitimate)".

**The structural half of the claim is true. The level is not what the paper says.**

- **Cell family: genuinely fixed in advance.** The lattice `lambda·P` and the
  in-region filter (`gate_density_step_t.py:129–131, 165–173`) depend only on
  `lambda = 0.25` and the region box `|x| <= 3, |v| <= 2`. No data enters. The
  `ALPHAS` grid is also hard-coded (`:263`). So the post-selection argument is the right
  one, and selecting `{c : bound >= alpha}` afterwards is legitimate — *given a
  simultaneous per-cell bound*.
- **But the per-cell level is a fixed `z = 4`, not `delta/K`.**
  `gate_density_step_t.py:215–219` sets `Z_CONSERVATIVE = 4.0` with the docstring claim
  "a per-cell level of ~3.2e-5; with a few thousand cells the family-wise level stays
  below `delta = 0.05`". One-sided level at `z = 4` is `3.1671e-5`, so that claim is
  true only up to **1578 cells**. Measured cell counts:

  | step | cells with a bound | family-wise level at `z = 4` |
  |---|---|---|
  | 2 | 404 | 0.0128 ✓ |
  | 5 | 1147 | 0.0363 ✓ |
  | 10 | 2281 | 0.0722 ✗ |
  | 20 | 4821 | 0.1527 ✗ |
  | 40 | 7361 | 0.2331 ✗ |
  | 80 | 8596 | 0.2722 ✗ |

  Against the a-priori in-region family (**9806 cells**, the honest denominator, since
  which cells receive a bound is itself data-dependent) the family-wise level is
  **0.311** — 6.2× over `delta`. The required `z` is **4.4129**.

- **Corrected level-set volumes** (recomputed at `z = 4.4129`, same 40,000 `W_t`
  samples, `step_t_wilson.paper_quoted_volumes`):

  | quoted in main.tex:377 | published (`z = 4`) | corrected (`z = 4.4129`) |
  |---|---|---|
  | step 20, `{p >= 0.05}` | 3.087 → "3.09" | **3.020** |
  | step 40, `{p >= 0.02}` | 6.1245 → "6.13" | **5.774** |
  | step 80, `{p >= 0.01}` | 6.2055 → "6.21" | **5.274** |

  The three volumes the paper quotes shrink by 2%, 6% and 15%. **No conclusion changes**:
  the step-`t` section's result is that `N = 40` certifies *nothing* on any of these
  level sets (`gate_density_step_t.json.best = null`), and smaller regions only reinforce
  that. But the numbers as printed are asserted at a confidence the code does not deliver.

- **A caveat on my own correction.** A Wilson interval's level is *nominal*: `Phi(-z)` is
  its asymptotic coverage, not a finite-sample guarantee. So `z = 4.4129` fixes the
  Bonferroni arithmetic but leaves the per-cell bound asymptotic. The fully rigorous
  version is a Clopper–Pearson lower bound per cell at `delta/9806`, which the codebase
  already declines for a stated reason (`gate_density_step_t.py:217–219`: the exact tail
  overflows in plain floats at `n` in the tens of thousands) — a reason that no longer
  applies, since the log-space tail used in this script handles `n = 20000` fine. That
  would be the clean fix if the step-`t` numbers are ever load-bearing; today they are not.

- **A second, currently vacuous selection layer.** `gate_density_step_t.py:298–307`
  minimises the uniform bound over all 36 `(step, alpha)` pairs. If any pair had
  certified, that would be a second uncorrected selection on top of the per-cell one.
  None does, so it costs nothing today — but it is a trap if the gate size is ever raised.

## 5. The 400-gate validation: is it circular?

**Yes, it uses the certificate's own partition, deliberately** —
`gate_partition_validation.py:45–53` reads `(n_y, n_v, n_a)` out of
`results/gate_partition_certificate.json` rather than re-deriving the grid, because the
previous generation of this pair shared an off-by-one grid bug and therefore validated
it (`:11–15`). That is the right call and the paper says so (main.tex:394).

For the purpose it is used for, the circularity is benign and in one respect is exactly
what is needed:

- **The rollouts are out of sample.** Validation seeds are `1_000_000*trial + r`
  (`:61`), i.e. `{0..39} ∪ {1000000..1000039} ∪ …`; the certificate's MC used
  `4242..24241`. **Overlap: 0 of 16,000 seeds** (`seed_hygiene.overlap_size`). So the
  400 gates are a genuinely fresh test of the *selected* partition — and the selected
  partition is precisely where selection-induced optimism would show up. This is the
  empirical answer to §2, and it agrees with the analytic one.
- **What it cannot do: resolve tightness.** `400/400` covered gives Wilson
  `[0, 0.0095]` against a bound of `0.0096`. The paper says as much ("the Hoeffding
  slack is finer than 400 trials can resolve"), so its "essentially exact" claim is
  correctly attributed to part (a) only. One refinement available there: the paper reads
  the `K = 8` result as "measured `0.0375` against the bound `0.0383` — tight to 2%".
  The exact probability is `0.038038`, so the union bound's own slack is `0.7%` and the
  remaining `1.4%` is 400-trial noise (the measurement's Wilson interval is
  `[0.0229, 0.0609]`, which contains the exact value). Attributing all 2% to the union
  bound slightly overstates how sharp 400 trials are.
- **What it does not test at all: `p_hat_C = 0.800`.** The coverage event and the `p_C`
  estimate are different quantities; a gate can cover 400/400 with `p_C` mis-estimated.

### 5b. Out-of-sample replication of `p_C` (new here)

Since the shared-sample worry is about `p_hat_C` and nothing in the paper re-measures it,
this audit re-ran the estimate for the selected `K = 36` partition on a **disjoint**
20,000-rollout stream (seeds `10 000 000 …`, disjoint from both `4242..24241` and the
validation's seeds). `out_of_sample_p_C`:

| | value |
|---|---|
| published worst `p_hat_C` | 0.80010 |
| reproduced on the published seeds | 0.80010 (exact match) |
| **fresh, disjoint 20,000 rollouts** | **0.79960** |
| absolute movement | 0.00050 |
| Hoeffding radius it is bounded by | 0.01385 |
| argmax cell, published vs fresh | `(2,0,3)` vs `(2,0,3)` — the same cell |
| `36 · (p_fresh + t)^40` | 0.00932 `<= delta/2` |

The estimate moves by 3.6% of the radius that bounds it, the binding cell is identical,
and the certificate holds on the fresh sample. Whatever else `0.800` is, it is not an
artifact of the shared stream.

## 6. A latent bug in the validation harness (not in the certificate)

`gate_partition_validation.py:72–73` breaks out of the step loop after the **first**
iteration, so the part-(a) regime samples `(x_0, v_0, a_0)`. But `v_0 == 0.0` exactly
(`CartWall.initial_state`, `envs.py:89–90`) — and the paper itself says so
(main.tex:429: "our gate's step-0 law is supported on a lower-dimensional set … `v = 0`
exactly"). The certificate's part (a) assumes the **step-1** law, uniform on the sheared
box (main.tex:337). The validation is therefore validating a different sample than the
certificate describes.

**It is benign here, and provably so.** With `n_v = 1` the velocity axis is a single
cell, so `v = 0` and `v ~ U(-V,V)` are indistinguishable; and `y_1 = x_1 - dt·v_1 = x_0 =
y_0` identically (verified to 1e-12) while `a_0` and `a_1` are i.i.d. uniform. So at
`n_v = 1` the step-0 and step-1 samples have the *same joint law* in the coordinates that
matter, and `385/400` is the right number.

**It would not be benign for any `n_v >= 2`.** Demonstrated over 200 trials
(`part_a_sample_index.demonstration`):

| split | `K` | cover rate as validated (step 0) | cover rate with the correct step-1 sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2×1×4 (the certified split) | 8 | 0.965 | 0.950 |
| 2×2×2 | 8 | **0.000** | 0.955 |
| 1×2×4 | 8 | **0.000** | 0.960 |

(The first row's two rates differ only by sampling noise on 200 trials: the *laws* are
identical, the *realisations* are not, since `a_0` and `a_1` are different draws from the
same stream. Both bracket the exact `1 - 0.038038 = 0.961962`.)

With `v == 0` the index `int((v+V)/dv)` lands in one v-cell forever, so the other
v-cells can never be hit and the harness reports 0/400 for a sound certificate. The fix
is one line — break after the second step, not the first — and it changes no published
number, but it should be fixed before anyone re-runs the certificate at a larger `N`
(where `n_v = 1` will stop being optimal).

## 7. Verdict and the sentence the paper should use

**Class (ii): rigorous modulo a Monte-Carlo confidence level that is correctly accounted
for in the code and not stated in the prose.** `K = 36`, `rho = 0.363`, `0.933`,
`L <= 5.77` all stand, at `1 - 5delta/6 = 0.9583`. Nothing needs relabelling as
"empirically calibrated". What is missing is disclosure: a reader of main.tex:347 cannot
tell (a) at what level Hoeffding was applied, (b) that it was Bonferroni'd over cells,
(c) that `K` was selected against the same sample over a family of candidates, or
(d) how `delta` was split.

Replacement for the sentence at main.tex:347 (from "the admissible partition grows" to
the display at :349):

> the finest partition the deployed gate certifies grows from $K = 8$ to $K = 36$. Here
> the certificate stops being sampling-free, so the accounting has to be explicit.
> $p_C$ is estimated on $20{,}000$ gate rollouts drawn once and shared by every
> candidate partition, while the candidate family itself is geometric and fixed before
> any rollout is drawn (one coarsest partition per target radius, $26$ of them), so the
> only data-dependent choice is which member is certified. Hoeffding is therefore
> applied at level $\delta/(3K)$ \emph{per cell}, so that $p_C \leq \hat p_C + t$ holds
> simultaneously across all $K$ cells with probability $1 - \delta/3$ and the selected
> worst cell needs no further correction; the remaining $\delta/2$ pays for the gate's
> own miss, $5\delta/6 \leq \delta$ in total. At $K = 36$ the worst cell is
> $\hat p_C = 0.800$, bounded above by $0.814$, giving $36 \cdot 0.814^{40} = 0.010 \leq
> \delta/2$ and
> \[ \rho = 0.363, \qquad \sup_U \|f - \hat f\|_\infty \leq \mathbf{0.933}. \]
> Selecting $K$ against the sample is a multiple comparison, and it is worth reporting
> that it costs nothing here: because Hoeffding's radius grows only like
> $\sqrt{\log(1/\text{level})}$, widening the Bonferroni correction from one partition's
> cells to \emph{every} cell of \emph{every} candidate in the family ($9674$ tests)
> raises the bound on the worst cell to $0.818$ and the failure probability to $0.012$,
> and selects the same $K = 36$; replacing Hoeffding by an exact-binomial
> Clopper--Pearson bound at the same level gives $0.809$ and also selects $K = 36$. So
> $\rho = 0.363$ is an artifact of neither the search nor the concentration inequality
> (\texttt{scripts/certificate\_simultaneity.py}).

And for main.tex:375, replace "a Wilson lower bound per cell at level $\delta/K$ over a
cell family fixed in advance (so selecting a level set afterwards is legitimate)" with:

> a Wilson lower bound per cell over a cell family fixed in advance --- the $9806$ cells
> of the lattice that lie inside the region box --- at the per-cell (nominal) level
> $\delta/9806$, i.e.\ $z = 4.41$, so the bounds hold simultaneously and selecting a level
> set afterwards is legitimate.

(The parenthetical "nominal" is doing work: a Wilson bound's level is asymptotic. If these
volumes ever become load-bearing the honest object is a Clopper–Pearson lower bound at the
same level.)

with the three quoted volumes at main.tex:377 changed to `3.02`, `5.77` and `5.27`.
Note that `scripts/audit_paper2_numbers.py` pins the current `3.087 / 6.125 / 6.206`
against `results/gate_density_step_t.json` (three `claim(...)` calls in the
"density at step t" block), so correcting the level would require re-running
`gate_density_step_t.py` at `z = 4.4129` and updating those claims together. This audit
deliberately did not touch either file; the corrected values live in
`results/certificate_simultaneity.json` under
`step_t_wilson.recomputed.<step>.levels.<alpha>.vol_corrected`.

Finally, one sentence to add to the "Is the bound tight?" paragraph (main.tex:394),
since the 400-gate test does not cover the estimated quantity:

> The estimate the certificate rests on is checked separately: re-measuring $p_C$ for the
> same $K = 36$ partition on a \emph{disjoint} $20{,}000$-rollout stream moves the worst
> cell from $0.8001$ to $0.7996$ --- $3.6\%$ of the Hoeffding radius that bounds it, with
> the same binding cell --- so the $0.814$ is slack in the inequality and not calibration
> to one sample.

### Residual caveats

- The audit reads the committed `params` block; it cannot see whether `--mc-rollouts`,
  `--seed` or the `0.08` sweep floor were themselves tuned, since inspecting git history
  was out of scope for this run. Nothing in the code or the JSONs suggests they were.
- `--max-cells-b` (default 400) is recorded in the certificate's `params` and never
  used; the family is capped by `rho > 0.08` and reaches `K = 2093`. A reader
  reproducing from the JSON would infer the wrong family. Cosmetic, but it is a
  reproducibility hazard in exactly the place this audit is about.
- Nothing in the certificate itself was changed. This audit is read-only on
  `scripts/gate_*.py`, `results/gate_*.json` and `docs/paper2/main.tex`.
- The 20,000 rollouts are i.i.d. only in the sense that `random.Random(seed + i)` for
  sequential `i` yields independent streams. That is the same assumption every other
  measurement in the paper makes, and the gate itself uses the identical construction
  (`gate.py:48–51`), so the estimate is of the right quantity — but it is an assumption
  about MT19937 seeding, not a theorem.

## 8. Reproducing this

```
PYTHONPATH=src .venv/bin/python scripts/certificate_simultaneity.py
```

Costs no network and no money: everything is re-derived from `results/gate_*.json` plus
CPU-only rollouts of `CartWall`. Roughly 8 minutes, checkpointed per unit into
`results/certificate_simultaneity.json` (atomic temp-file + `os.replace`); the step-`t`
Monte Carlo and the out-of-sample `p_C` replication are skipped if already present, so a
restart is cheap. `--skip-step-t` skips the 40,000-sample `W_t` recomputation.

The script ends in a block of self-checks and **exits non-zero if any fails**, so it acts
as a regression test on this document's claims rather than merely printing them. The
checks that matter are the ones that would go vacuous if the audit were wrong:

- the brute-force 200,000-trial coupon simulation must agree with the exact
  inclusion-exclusion probability to within 4 sigma (`K = 8` and `K = 9`);
- the union bound must dominate the exact probability at every `K` examined;
- Hoeffding's closed-form radius must equal an independent bisection solve, and must be
  conservative against the exact binomial tail at the operating point;
- the family-wise correction must actually *move* the `p_C` bound (otherwise
  "K = 36 survives it" proves nothing), and at least one evaluated candidate must have
  been rejected (otherwise the selection rule was never binding);
- the step-index demonstration must show `0.000` coverage at `n_v >= 2` with the step-0
  sample *and* `> 0.9` with the correct step-1 sample (otherwise the defect is not
  exercised);
- the step-`t` reimplementation must reproduce every published level-set cell count at
  `z = 4` before its corrected numbers are believed;
- the out-of-sample `p_C` must differ from the published one (a fresh sample that
  reproduced it bit-for-bit would mean the seeds were not disjoint after all).

`self_checks` / `self_checks_all_pass` in the JSON record the outcome.
